What's Working — the Outlook is the strongest letter in the stack:
  • "Key Takeaways" are excellent and consistent. Three numbered takeaways at the top of every issue. Opinionated, data-backed, and each points to a specific section for details. This is the editorial voice the LWID should copy.
  • Structured sections are standardized. 1A Macro → 1B Capital On-Ramp → 1C Week-Ahead Catalysts → Section 2 Risk Regime → Section 3 Chain Comparison → Section 4 Protocol Discovery. Readers know exactly where to find what they need.
  • Regime verdicts are genuinely useful. Aug 4: "Dollar liquidity left the two largest stablecoins at the fastest weekly pace of the quarter." Jul 28: "RISK-ON. Three things changed this week, all in the same direction." These are clear, actionable reads.
  • Chain-by-chain stablecoin flow analysis is unique — no other DeFi newsletter does this with this level of granularity. Monad, Robinhood Chain, Solana each get individual narrative + data.
  • Token Price Movers with "observations, not trade ideas" framing — credible, avoids liability, still useful.
  • Week-Ahead Catalysts section — gives readers a forward calendar. Rare in crypto newsletters.
What Needs Improvement:
  • Word count is extreme. Jul 21: 4,252 words. Aug 18: 3,029 words — still over the 2,500 target but below the Jul 21 peak. Content quality is high but length is a retention problem.
  • Section 4 (Protocol Discovery) has no length discipline. Ranges from 846 to 4,098 words. When a protocol warrants 500+ words, it should be a Friday Guide.
  • No cross-links. Jul 28 discusses Solana yield venues (Loopscale, Exponent) — the same venues covered in the upcoming Friday Guide. Zero reference.
  • Premium CTA disappeared. Jul 28, Aug 4, and Aug 18 have no premium pitch. The Outlook audience is the most analytically sophisticated segment — highest conversion potential.
  • Duplicate content across sections. Aug 4: Monad’s Aave incentives described in both Section 3A and Section 4. Jul 21: Robinhood Chain analysis split across Sections 2, 3, and 4.
  • Duplicate headers. Jul 28: “Driver two: the switch” appears twice. Copy-paste artifacts that erode trust.
  • Token Price Movers floats. Appears between Key Takeaways and 1A in Aug 4, inside Section 4 in Jul 28, in Section 4A in Jun 23. Pick one position.
  • News Headlines block at the top delays the Key Takeaways. Consider moving below KT or removing (readers get daily news from the daily letter).

5. Recommended Tweet Strategy

The Outlook's content is data-rich, which makes it ideal for the formats that actually work on Twitter: surprising numbers and named catalysts. Aug 18, Aug 25, and Sep 1 all hit 3 tweets per issue — the framework below is now being executed consistently.

3-Tweet Framework for Each Outlook

@todayindefi Tuesday evening UTC+8 — Data Hook

The single most surprising number from this week's data. Lead with the stat, add one line of "why it matters," withhold the explanation.

Format: [Surprising $ stat]. [One-line implication]. Full breakdown: [link]

Model tweet: "Solana pulled in $1.19B of stablecoins last week — more than every other chain combined that we tracked." (2,197 imps)

@todayindefi Tuesday evening UTC+8 — Protocol Catalyst

Name the one protocol with the most interesting catalyst this week. Use the "nobody's talking about" or "catalyst still ahead" frame.

Format: [Protocol]'s [catalyst] may be [bigger/more important] than [what everyone's watching]. Details: [link]

Model tweet: "The $HYPE catalyst nobody's talking about? Institutional rails. Real trader incentives. A buyback loop tied to usage." (14,455 imps)

@todayindefi Wednesday morning UTC+8 — Regime Verdict

The Outlook's weekly regime call, condensed to one tweet. This is the letter's unique value prop — no one else publishes a weekly onchain risk-on/risk-off verdict.

Format: This week's onchain verdict: [RISK-ON/OFF/STABILIZING]. [1-2 sentence reasoning]. Full analysis: [link]

Why this works: Creates a recurring franchise tweet. Readers come back weekly for the call. Validated: the Aug 26 "RISK-ON" verdict tweet hit 2,666 impressions — 3rd best Outlook tweet ever. Keep this format every Wednesday.

6. Action Items (Priority Order)

Headlines

1. Kill "- Onchain Outlook" suffix and cut to <55 chars. Lead with the single most surprising data point. One hook, not two. The best performers (21%+ OR) all had one clear, dramatic stat in the headline.
2. Try question format. "What Drove Solana to Lead Inflows?" was the closest attempt (Jul 28) but was buried after the suffix. Flip it: make the question the entire headline. The Mar 18 tweet proves questions drive 13x engagement.

Content & Click-Through

3. Add actionable app links to every chain section. The click data is unambiguous: app.aave.com (32 clicks), Sphere Markets (29), Merkl (12). When readers can click to do something, they do. The Aug 4 issue had zero app links and got 36 total clickers vs 52 on the Jul 21 issue. Add at least one "try this" link per chain in Section 3.
4. Link every data point to its source. The high-click issue linked CryptoQuant, DefiLlama, TradingView, CoinGecko, Treasury.gov inline. The low-click issue paraphrased more and linked less. Same content quality, 44% fewer clicks. The Outlook works as a curated discovery layer — lean into it.
5. Embed more charts. Images/charts get 26–30 clicks each (readers enlarging). More visual data = more engagement = higher sponsor clicks too (DeFi Saver: 7 in sparse issue vs 32 in chart-heavy issue).
6. Cap total word count at 2,500. The Outlook has nearly doubled from March (1,749) to Jul–Aug (3,250 avg). Aug 18 came in at 3,029 — below the Jul 21 peak but still 20% over target. The workbook budget is ~2,200. The Key Takeaways, Macro, and Capital On-Ramp sections are the core value. Protocol Discovery (Section 4) should be capped at 3 protocols / 400 words, or spun into the Friday Guide when a protocol warrants 1,000+ words.
7. Fix duplicate sections. Jul 28: "Driver two: the switch" header appears twice. Aug 4: Monad Aave incentives described in both Section 3A and Section 4. These are copy-paste artifacts that erode trust.
8. Cross-link to other letters. When the Outlook covers yield venues (Loopscale, Exponent, Kamino), link to the relevant Farming or Guide letter. When it covers exploits, link to the LWID. The Outlook generates the thesis; other letters provide the execution detail.
9. Restore the premium CTA. Jul 28 through Sep 1 have no premium pitch — five consecutive issues without one. The Outlook audience is the most analytically sophisticated segment — highest conversion potential for premium.

Tweets

10. Maintain 3 tweets per Outlook. Aug 18, Aug 25, and Sep 1 all hit 3 tweets — up from the 1.5 average earlier. Keep this cadence. The data hook + protocol catalyst + regime verdict framework is producing consistent results.
11. Lead every tweet with a specific number or protocol name. Tweets starting with abstract macro commentary ("Risk sentiment is improving", "Everything bled") average 439 impressions. Tweets with a $ figure or protocol name average 2,700+. The ratio is 6:1.
12. Keep the "Weekly Verdict" tweet franchise going. The Aug 26 RISK-ON verdict tweet (2,666 impressions, 1.61% ER) validated this format. It's the 3rd-best Outlook tweet ever. Post the regime call every Wednesday morning as a standalone franchise tweet — it's now a proven format.
