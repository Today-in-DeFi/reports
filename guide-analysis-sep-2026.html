Headline Analysis

What works (20%+ OR)

1. Curiosity + contrarian framing. “DeFi Tokens the Market May Have Mispriced” (21.2%) — the question mark + “mispriced” implies the reader knows something others don’t. This was the best Guide headline of the period.
2. Outcome + named protocols. “Amplifying Stablecoin LP APY with Leverage: Tangent and LlamaLend v2” (21.0%) — tells you the result (amplify APY) and names specific protocols so it doesn’t feel generic.
3. News-hook + number. “Ethereum Glamsterdam is Coming: Mainnet Fees Could Drop 3x” (20.7%) — combines a timely event with a concrete magnitude. Reads like breaking analysis, not an explainer.

What doesn’t work (sub-19.5% OR)

1. Generic “Here’s How” framing. “Tokenized Stocks Can Now Earn You Yield — Here’s How” (18.4%) — no urgency, no number, no edge. “Here’s How” is the most overused CTA in newsletters and signals low-effort content.
2. Too-long headlines that truncate. “After $116M Vanished From ‘Secure’ Wallets: The Actual Checklist for Safe Self-Custody” (18.7%) — 86 characters, most inboxes cut at ~60. The strongest part (“$116M Vanished”) shows but the payoff (“Checklist for Safe Self-Custody”) doesn’t.
3. Product-listing headlines with niche names. “DeFi Fixed Rate Loans: Lock in 2-3% Borrow on Mopho Midnight, Tenor, & TermMax” (19.6%) — three unknown protocol names and a typo (“Mopho”). Reads like an ad, not editorial. Also truncates at 80 chars.

Length doesn’t cause low opens directly (readers can’t see word count before opening) — but longer Guides tend to have more technical, niche headlines that signal “this will be a heavy read.”

Content structure observations

TL;DR adoption is working. All recent Guides include a TL;DR box at the top with 4–5 bullets (what, why now, best opportunity, biggest risk, who it’s for). This is good — keep it. The best TL;DRs read like a standalone briefing, not a table of contents.
Topic diversity is the Guide’s strength. The 11 issues span: yield strategies (stablecoins, LPs, options), protocol deep-dives (Panoptic, Tangent, LlamaLend), ecosystem maps (perps dexes, Solana yields), security (self-custody), macro/valuation (mispriced tokens), and new primitives (tokenized stocks, fixed-rate loans). No other letter covers this range. Lean into this — it’s what makes the Guide distinct.
Best-performing issues share a common structure:
  1. Open with the “hidden truth” — the non-obvious insight that justifies the Guide existing (e.g., “most tokens are down 40–70% but some are still earning”)
  2. Framework or filter — give the reader a decision tool, not just information
  3. Specific opportunities — name venues, rates, links
  4. Risk section — honest, quantitative
Weakest issues skip the “why now” hook. The Self-Custody guide (18.7% OR) opened with background on the Coldcard hack rather than leading with “your seed may be compromised right now.” The Covered Calls guide (19.8% OR) opened with a textbook definition of options rather than the practical edge. Frontload the urgency.

General content recommendations

#RecommendationWhy
1 Open with the contrarian insight, not the explainer. First 2 sentences should be: “here’s what the market is getting wrong” or “here’s the window that just opened.” Top 3 Guides all led with a market observation, not a definition
2 Target 2,000–2,800 words. Enough depth to justify the “guide” label, short enough to not signal “homework.” Sweet-spot bucket averaged 20.2% OR; 4,500+ word issues averaged 19.3%
3 End with “What to do right now” — a 3–4 bullet action box, not a risk disclaimer. Readers who scroll to the end are the most engaged — give them a clear next step
4 Cross-link to Farming letter when the Guide covers a yield strategy. 6 of 11 Guides were yield-adjacent — link to that week’s specific farms
5 Add a “who is this for” qualifier in the TL;DR or opening paragraph. Guide topics vary widely — letting readers self-select reduces unsubscribe risk on niche topics

Twitter patterns that work

1. Highest engagement rate: “Direct benefit” framing (avg 4.2% ER).
“Your Curve LP shouldn’t just sit there” (6.58% ER) and “Most DeFi yield comes from the same crowded trades” (3.51% ER). These tell the reader their current behavior is leaving money on the table.
2. Highest impressions: Fear/news hooks (avg 2,590 imp).
“$116M drained from hardware wallets” (2,590 imp) and “A carry trade only works if your cost of funds is known” (3,651 imp with image). Threat-first tweets get amplified, even when the Guide OR was lower.
3. Monday tweets outperform Friday tweets.
Across all 9 Guides with tweet data, the Monday follow-up tweet averaged 1,330 impressions vs. 770 for Friday evening tweets. The Covered Calls Monday tweet (2,844 imp) beat both Friday tweets (579 + 719) combined. Weekend readers discover the letter and are primed for the reminder.

Twitter patterns that don’t work

1. Repeating the headline verbatim. Friday tweets that just restated the subject line averaged 0.6% ER — readers who skipped the email won’t click the same words on Twitter. Reframe the angle.
2. Two similar tweets on the same day. Several Guides had two Friday tweets with near-identical framing. The second consistently got 40–60% fewer impressions. Use the second slot for a different angle (e.g., if tweet 1 is the insight, tweet 2 is a specific number or risk callout).
