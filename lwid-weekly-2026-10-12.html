Actions for the Next LWID

  1. Lead with forward-looking threats or catalysts, never resolved events. “Blast Winding Down” tells the reader the story is over. Reframe as: “Blast Is Shutting Down — What Happens to Locked Funds?” Even a concluded story can point forward if you highlight what’s unresolved.
  2. Use “how it was caught” framing for security stories. The NEAR attacker-identification angle generated the best engagement of the Oct 5 tweet set and the Blockaid thread dominated newsletter clicks. The LWID audience wants to understand security mechanisms, not just outcomes.
  3. Cut to ~2,500 words. Three consecutive issues above 3,000 words with declining ORs. Move lower-priority stories into a “Worth watching” bullet section at the bottom.
  4. Add one cross-link to Thursday’s Farming Alpha or Wednesday’s Airdrop Alpha. The Fables TGE link (5 clicks) proves LWID readers engage with airdrop content. Formalize this with a cross-link line near the CTA.
  5. Use one narrative tweet and one knowledge-gap tweet. The NEAR tweet worked because it had a twist (“identified in under a day”). Pair this type with a knowledge-gap tweet (“Everyone checks X. Almost nobody checks Y.”) which produced the all-time best LWID tweet in Sep 14 (2,219 imp, 11 clicks).
  6. Track OR against the 22.47% baseline. Three consecutive declines is a trend, not noise. If Oct 12 also misses, reassess the two-topic headline format entirely.
