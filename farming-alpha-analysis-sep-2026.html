Headline Rules for Farming Alpha

  1. Lead with context, not yield. "Base rates are 3%" sets up why 11% matters. Raw yield numbers without context are losing impact through repetition. The Aug 13 headline (21.98%) proves this works.
  2. Add urgency or scarcity. "Lock in" (Sep 10, 20.23%) outperformed "Lend GHO" (Sep 17, 18.77%) by 1.46pp. Imply time pressure: "before caps fill," "lock in," "while rates last."
  3. Drop niche protocol/token names. GHO, Loopscale, Balancer, and Morpho in headlines average 19.41% OR. Use asset type (stablecoins, ETH) or chain name (Ethereum, Arbitrum) instead.
  4. Keep yields above 10% in the headline. Sep 17 led with 8% APR — below the psychological threshold. If the best farm pays 8%, frame it as "nearly 2x the base rate" instead of the raw number.
  5. One hook, under 55 chars. Stop packing two ideas into one headline. The second is always truncated.
  6. Vary the template every week. Rotate between at least 3 formats: context+contrast, urgency framing, and dual-asset framing.

What's Working:
  • Farm cards with risk ratings remain a genuine differentiator. Each farm gets a protocol name, APR/APY, chain, strategy type, yield source breakdown, and a risk rating (Lower/Medium/High) with specific warnings. No other DeFi newsletter does this.
  • "Farming Benchmarks" section is excellent. Setting the baseline (3–5% unincentivized lending) gives readers context for why a 10%+ farm is noteworthy. This is the same pattern that made the Aug 13 headline work.
  • 3 farms per issue is the right number. Enough variety without overwhelming. Each farm gets adequate analysis depth.
  • Yield source breakdowns build trust. Readers can see exactly where the APR comes from (lending rate + incentives + boost) instead of a single number.
  • Risk warnings are specific and useful. Not generic disclaimers — actual protocol-specific risks (smart contract, impermanent loss, depeg scenarios).
What Needs Improvement:
  • Premium section is still bolted-on and unrelated. All three recent issues append an unrelated strategy guide as premium content. This should be a farming-specific deep-dive that extends one of the featured farms, not a standalone tutorial.
  • No cross-links. When a farm card features a protocol also covered in the Outlook (Aave, Pendle), there's no link to the deeper analysis. The Outlook covers the macro thesis; Farming Alpha covers the execution. Link them.
  • No week-over-week yield tracking. Readers cannot see if a farm's APR went up or down from last week. Adding a simple directional arrow or prior-week comparison would add recurring value and give readers a reason to open every week.
  • Section order should be locked. Benchmarks → Farm Cards → optional News. This order mirrors how the best headline (Aug 13) works: set context, then deliver the farms.

2. High impressions do not equal clicks. The @DeFiSaver incentive tweet (1,226 imps) and the "Earn 5% APY + $INK points" tweet (916 imps) both got 0 clicks. Protocol news and point-farming tweets get attention but don't drive subscription conversions. The audience engages but doesn't click through — likely because the tweet gives away the full info.
3. Sep 17 tweets averaged 776 impressions and 1.3 clicks — the weakest tweet set of the month. "Farm 5% ETH LP APR" (733 imps, 1 click) and "Lend GHO at 8% APR" (678 imps, 3 clicks) both lead with sub-10% yields. 4. The "2 farms worth knowing" format still wins on impressions but not clicks. 994 avg impressions across 3 uses, but only 0.7 avg clicks. It gives away all the info upfront. To convert impressions to clicks: list 2 farms but withhold the risk ratings and the third farm.

"GHO" as a token name in tweets performs poorly. "Lend GHO at 8% APR on an underrated chain" (678 imps) is the second-lowest in the Sep dataset. Farming Alpha tweets should be the most actionable content on the TID Twitter account. The audience is yield-seeking and impatient. Lead with numbers above 10%, name protocols with @tags, and withhold risk details to drive clicks. Currently averaging 3 tweets per issue (up from 2) — maintain this cadence with distinct angles.

Tactical Rules
