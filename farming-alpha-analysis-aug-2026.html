Headline Rules for Farming Alpha

  1. Lead with context, not yield. "Base rates are 3%" sets up why 11% matters. Raw yield numbers without context are losing impact through repetition.
  2. Drop niche protocol names. "Loopscale" and "Balancer" mean nothing to 80% of the list. Use asset type (stablecoins, ETH) or chain name (Ethereum, Arbitrum) instead.
  3. One hook, under 55 chars. Stop packing two ideas into one headline. The second is always truncated.
  4. Vary the template every week. The "Earn X% on Risk-Adjusted Stablecoins" formula has been used 7 times in 12 weeks. Even at 20% OR, it's declining.
  5. Drop the "Farming Alpha" / "- Farming Alpha" suffix. Readers know which letter this is from the sender name and schedule. Save 16–18 characters.

The two longest issues (Jul 16: 2,462 words; Aug 20: 2,206 words) are also among the worst performers. The two best openers (Aug 13: 1,032 words; Jun 18: 1,197 words) are the shortest. Correlation is not causation — headlines drive opens, not word count — but the pattern reinforces that Farming Alpha works best as a tight, focused letter.

What's Working:
  • Farm cards with risk ratings are a genuine differentiator. Each farm gets a protocol name, APR/APY, chain, strategy type, yield source breakdown, and a risk rating (Lower/Medium/High) with specific warnings. No other DeFi newsletter does this.
  • "Farming Benchmarks" section is excellent. Setting the baseline (3–5% unincentivized lending) gives readers context for why a 10%+ farm is noteworthy. This is the same pattern that made the Aug 13 headline work.
  • 3 farms per issue is the right number. Enough variety without overwhelming. Each farm gets adequate analysis depth.
  • Yield source breakdowns build trust. Readers can see exactly where the APR comes from (lending rate + incentives + boost) instead of a single number.
  • Risk warnings are specific and useful. Not generic disclaimers — actual protocol-specific risks (smart contract, impermanent loss, depeg scenarios).
What Needs Improvement:
  • Section order is inconsistent. Aug 20: Benchmarks → Farms → News. Aug 13: News → Benchmarks → Farms. Pick one order and stick with it. Readers build muscle memory — shuffling sections breaks scanning habits.
  • Premium section is bolted-on and unrelated. Both Aug 20 and Aug 13 append a "leveraged loop guide" as the premium section. This is the same Guide/LWID bundling problem — a 500-word strategy guide doesn't belong at the bottom of a farm-card letter. It should be a standalone Guide post or part of a farming-specific premium tier.
  • Word count doubles when news is added. Aug 20 (2,206 words) vs Aug 13 (1,032 words). The difference is the "This Week's Farming News" section expanding and extra news headlines. The farm cards themselves are consistently ~800–1,000 words. Everything else is filler.
  • No cross-links. When a farm card features a protocol also covered in the Outlook (Pendle, Aave), there's no link to the deeper analysis. The Outlook covers the macro thesis; Farming Alpha covers the execution. Link them.
  • No "week-over-week" yield tracking. Readers can't see if a farm's APR went up or down from last week. Adding a simple directional arrow or prior-week comparison would add recurring value.

Key Findings

1. The "2 farms worth knowing" format is the winner. Three uses, 994 avg impressions, 2.74% avg ER. It works because: numbered list creates scannable structure, @tags get protocol engagement, specific yields give the reader actionable info upfront. The Aug 7 version hit 1,199 impressions. The Jul 24 version (668) underperformed — likely because StakeDAO and Euler are less followed than AerodromeFi.
2. Named brands + specific rates outperform unnamed descriptions. "Robinhood Earn ~7%" (Jul 17, 1,115 imps) vs "A low-risk lending venue on Monad offering 6%" (Aug 4, 738 imps). Same idea, but naming the brand adds 51% more impressions. Protocol @tags also help the tweet reach the protocol's followers.
3. Vague/philosophical tweets fail. "Most high-APR pools are token emissions" (861 imps, 0.81% ER) and "Not every stablecoin farm is worth the risk" (730 imps, 0.68% ER) are the worst performers. Farming readers want actionable yield info, not commentary.
4. Chain-specific framing gets the highest engagement rate. "Two 11%+ stablecoin farms on Ethereum" (Jul 31, 4.16% ER) is the top ER in the dataset. Naming the chain creates a target audience filter — Ethereum farmers immediately self-select.
Click-through is weak across all formats. Average 2.8 clicks per tweet. Even the best tweets (1,199 imps) only got 1 click. The "2 farms worth knowing" format gives away enough information that readers may not need to click. Consider withholding one detail (the third farm, the risk rating, the chain) to create a click incentive.

4. Recommended Tweet Strategy

Farming Alpha tweets should be the most actionable content on the TID Twitter account. The audience is yield-seeking and impatient. Lead with numbers, name protocols, and @tag everything. Currently averaging 2 tweets per issue — increase to 3 with distinct angles.

3-Tweet Framework for Each Farming Alpha

@todayindefi Wednesday evening UTC+8 — Farm List

The "2 farms worth knowing" format. Numbered list, @protocol tags, specific APR, chain name. This is the proven winner (994 avg imps).

Format: 2 stablecoin farms worth knowing this week: 1) @Protocol — X% APR on [Chain] 2) @Protocol — Y% APR on [Chain] Risk ratings + full breakdown: [link]

Key change: Withhold the third farm and the risk ratings to incentivize the click. Current tweets give away all the info upfront (avg 2.8 clicks).

@todayindefi Wednesday evening UTC+8 — Context Hook

The "base rates are X%, but we found Y%" format. Sets context, then delivers the contrast. Models the Aug 13 headline that hit 20.51% OR.

Format: [Baseline context]. [Contrast with this week's farms]. [Link]

Example: "Unincentivized stablecoin lending is paying 3–4%. This week's Farming Alpha found 3 positions paying 10–15% with risk ratings for each: [link]"

@todayindefi Thursday morning UTC+8 — Single Farm Deep-Dive

One farm, explained in 2–3 lines. Name the protocol, the chain, the yield source breakdown. @tag the protocol. End with a risk hint that makes the reader click.

Format: @Protocol is paying X% APR on [asset] via [chain]. Here's where the yield comes from: — A% from [source 1] — B% from [source 2] The risk? [One-line tease]: [link]

Why this works: The yield breakdown adds credibility, but withholding the full risk assessment drives the click. This format also gets protocol retweets.

Tactical Rules

  1. Always @tag protocols. Tweets with @tags average 994 imps vs 754 without. The protocols retweet and their followers discover TID.
  2. Always include specific yield numbers. "15.74% via StakeDAO" beats "a high-yield farm." Every time.
  3. Name the chain. "on Ethereum" or "on Arbitrum" creates audience self-selection and boosts ER (Jul 31: 4.16% ER with "on Ethereum").
  4. Withhold one detail to drive clicks. Give the yield and protocol, but withhold the risk rating or the third farm. Current tweets give everything away.
  5. Never tweet vague farming commentary. "Most high-APR pools are token emissions" (0.81% ER) and "Not every farm is worth the risk" (0.68% ER) are the worst performers. The audience wants alpha, not wisdom.
