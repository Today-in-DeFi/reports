Why This Headline Failed

The number was right, the framing was wrong. $770B is genuinely shocking — it dwarfed Nasdaq’s daily volume. But the headline treated it as a fact report (“did $770B”) rather than a mystery (“how did a DEX nobody’s heard of beat Nasdaq?”). The Derive tweet proved the mystery framing works on this audience: “volume rose 6x — the reason why” got 6,834 impressions the same week.

Suggested Rewrites

Oct 12 original (17.42%):

Papertrade Did >$770B Volume in 2 Days

Rewrites:

A DEX Nobody’s Heard Of Just Beat Nasdaq’s Daily Volume

Withholds the name, leads with the impossibility. Creates a “wait, what?” response. 52 chars.

$770B in 2 Days — How a New Perp DEX Outpaced Nasdaq

Leads with the number, frames it as a mechanism story. 51 chars.

One DEX Did More Volume Than Nasdaq. Here’s the Catch

Comparison hook + “the catch” implies a twist worth opening for. 52 chars.

Actions for the Next LWID

  1. Never lead with an unknown protocol name. “Papertrade” failed because readers filter on recognition. If the story is about an unknown entity, lead with the mechanism or comparison instead: “A DEX Nobody’s Heard Of Just Beat Nasdaq’s Daily Volume.” Save the protocol name for the body.
  2. Use comparison framing in both headlines and tweets. The DeFi-vs-Bitcoin tweet (709 imp, 4 clicks) had the best click rate of the three tweets. The Papertrade tweet that said “flipped Nasdaq’s volume” outperformed the newsletter headline’s bare number. Comparisons create instant context that unknown names can’t.
  3. Three LWID tweets is working — keep it. Impressions up 51%, clicks up 167%, CTR up from 0.19% to 0.33%. The third tweet (comparison framing) added the most click value.
  4. Add a cross-link to Airdrop Alpha. Variational points (8 clicks) and last week’s Fables TGE (5 clicks) confirm LWID readers consistently engage with airdrop content. Add one line near the end: “Wednesday’s Airdrop Alpha covers these opportunities in detail.”
  5. OR trend is now critical. LWID open rates: 21.56% → 20.66% → 17.42% over three consecutive issues. If next week also misses 20%, the headline strategy needs a full reset — not incremental tweaks. Test the comparison/mystery format next Monday.
