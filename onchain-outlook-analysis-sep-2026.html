What's Working — the analysis letter remains the strongest in the stack:
  • "Key Takeaways" are excellent and consistent. Three numbered takeaways at the top of every issue. Opinionated, data-backed, and each points to a specific section for details. Seven consecutive issues with no deviation.
  • Structured sections are standardized. Macro → Capital On-Ramp → Week-Ahead Catalysts → Risk Regime → Chain Comparison → Protocol Discovery. Readers know exactly where to find what they need.
  • The name shift is working. Dropping “Onchain Outlook” from headlines freed up 18 chars for actual content. Sep headlines are shorter, punchier, and — with two exceptions — better-performing.
  • Regime verdicts continue to deliver unique editorial value. No other DeFi newsletter publishes a weekly onchain risk-on/risk-off verdict with this level of data backing.
  • Chain-by-chain stablecoin flow analysis remains unique — granular coverage of Robinhood Chain, Plasma, Monad, Solana with individual narrative + data.
What Needs Improvement:
  • Cross-links still absent. Seven consecutive issues with zero cross-references to other letters. When Sep 1 covers Plasma yield strategies, it should link to the Farming letter. When Sep 15 covers AI tokens, it should link to the relevant Protocol Guide. The analysis letter generates the thesis; other letters provide execution detail.
  • Premium CTA still missing. Seven consecutive issues with no premium pitch. The analysis audience is the most analytically sophisticated segment — highest conversion potential. This is leaving revenue on the table.
  • Delivers declining. From 4,705 (Aug 4) to 4,636 (Sep 15) — a loss of 69 subscribers in 6 weeks. The paid-only audience is shrinking. Premium CTA absence may be contributing.
  • Sep 15 topic selection was too abstract. “AI Usage Is Turning Revenue Into Token Burns” names no protocol and offers no specific number. The audience opens for specifics, not themes. If the best topic of the week is conceptual, the headline must still name one protocol and one number.

The Name Shift: “Onchain Outlook” to General Analysis

Sep confirmed the name change is a net positive. The three Sep issues dropped the “Onchain Outlook” prefix entirely. Results: Sep 1 (20.97%), Sep 8 (20.72%), Sep 15 (19.41%). The average (20.37%) beats the Jun–Jul Outlook average (19.70%) by 0.67pp. The Sep 15 underperformance is a headline/topic problem, not a name problem — the two best Sep headlines both lack the prefix and hit 20.7%+ OR. The suffix/prefix was costing 18 chars and signaling nothing the reader didn't already know.

Key Findings

1. Tangible asset references drive clicks. The two highest-click tweets (7 each) both reference real-world assets: “a real Nasdaq stock” (Sep 8) and “NVDA and PFE” (Sep 15). When readers see something they recognize from traditional finance repurposed in DeFi, they click.
2. The Sep 7 Swiss Franc tweet is an outlier in engagement (69 eng, 2,412 imp) but low clicks (4). High engagement, low click-through = the tweet itself was the content. Readers liked/retweeted the take but didn't need to read more. For newsletter promotion, this is a vanity metric — engagement without conversion. The Sep 8 “meme coin” tweet had lower engagement (20) but higher clicks (7) — that's the better promo tweet.
3. "Catalyst of the week" framing produces 0–1 clicks consistently. Sep 8's edgeX catalyst tweet: 1 click. Sep 15's Renzo catalyst tweet: 0 clicks. This framing signals news, not insight. The audience doesn't click on announcements. Retire this framing.
4. Recommendations from Aug report still apply. Add actionable app links to every chain section. Link every data point to its source. Embed more charts. The high-click issues in Jul–Aug had 52 clickers (dense links) vs 36 (sparse links). Same content quality, 44% fewer clicks.

5. Recommended Tweet Strategy

Sep data confirms and refines the 3-tweet framework. The main update: retire "catalyst of the week" framing and replace with a new format. The audience clicks on absurd claims and TradFi-DeFi bridges, not news announcements.

Updated 3-Tweet Framework

@todayindefi Tuesday 21:00 UTC+8 — Absurd Claim / Data Hook

The single most surprising or counterintuitive finding from the letter. Lead with the claim, withhold the explanation.

Format: [Absurd/counterintuitive stat]. [One line of implication]. Full analysis: [link]

Model tweets: "A meme coin moved a real Nasdaq stock 71% in a session" (7 clicks, 2.51% ER) / "$4.8M TVL DEX did $6B volume" (1.96% ER)

@todayindefi Tuesday 22:00 UTC+8 — TradFi Bridge / Yield Hook

Make a DeFi opportunity concrete by naming a real-world asset or yield the reader already understands. This replaces the underperforming “catalyst of the week” slot.

Format: [TradFi asset/ticker] + [DeFi mechanism] = [concrete outcome]. Details: [link]

Model tweet: "Earn dividends on NVDA and PFE without owning a share. Pendle tokenized stock dividends" (7 clicks, 2.53% ER)

Replaces: "Catalyst of the week: [protocol] [news]" — 0.5 avg clicks, 1.54% ER. Dead format.

@todayindefi Wednesday 09:30 UTC+8 — Regime Verdict

The letter's weekly regime call, condensed to one tweet. Recurring franchise — readers come back weekly for the call.

Format: This week's onchain verdict: [RISK-ON/OFF/STABILIZING]. [1-2 sentence reasoning]. Full analysis: [link]

Why this works: Aug 26 RISK-ON verdict: 2,666 impressions, 1.61% ER. Remains the 3rd-best Outlook tweet ever. This is a proven franchise format — keep it every Wednesday morning.

6. Action Items (Priority Order)

Headlines

1. Keep the curiosity/contrarian formula but require a named entity. Sep 15 proves the formula fails without a specific protocol or number. The rule: every headline must contain either (a) a named protocol/chain or (b) a specific dollar/percentage figure — ideally both. “AI Usage Is Turning Revenue Into Token Burns” has neither. “One AI Token Is Burning $2M/mo From Real Revenue” has both.
2. Single-hook headlines only. Sep 1 (60 chars, two hooks) still worked because the first hook was exceptionally strong, but the second hook is invisible in most inboxes. Sep 8 (47 chars, one hook, 20.72%) and Aug 25 (46 chars, one hook, 20.08%) confirm: one strong claim > two diluted claims.
3. Avoid abstract topic framing. Sep 15 (“AI Usage”, 19.41%) and Jul 28 (“What Drove Solana”, 19.41%) are the two lowest-performing issues in 4 months. Both describe themes rather than events. When the week’s best story is conceptual, ground it in one protocol’s specific numbers.

Content & Click-Through

4. Implement the “This Week’s Signal” section. Recommended in Aug, still not added. The analysis letter’s best insight gets buried across Sections 2–4. Pulling it up front gives readers the editorial thesis immediately. One section, 400 words, the single most important onchain development of the week.
5. Add actionable app links to every chain section. Still the #1 click driver from Jul data (Aave app: 32 clicks, Sphere: 29, Merkl: 12). When readers can click to do something, they do. Seven Sep issues without this change.
6. Add cross-links to other letters. Seven consecutive issues with zero cross-references. When the analysis letter covers a protocol that has a Guide or Farming writeup, link to it. This drives engagement across the newsletter stack and reinforces the value of the paid subscription.
7. Restore the premium CTA. Seven consecutive issues without a premium pitch. The analysis audience is the most analytically sophisticated segment. Every issue should include one premium callout, positioned after the first content section.

Tweets

8. Retire “catalyst of the week” permanently. edgeX (Sep 8): 1 click. Renzo (Sep 15): 0 clicks. Two consecutive weeks, two failures, combined 1 click on 1,614 impressions. Replace with the TradFi Bridge / Yield Hook format: name a real-world asset, show the DeFi mechanism, make it actionable. The Pendle/NVDA tweet (7 clicks, 2.53% ER) is the model.
9. Maintain 3 tweets per issue. Sep averaged 3.0 tweets/issue. The cadence is right. Engagement rate improved from 1.52% (Aug) to 1.91% (Sep). The quality of hooks is improving. Keep the absurd claim + TradFi bridge + regime verdict framework.
10. Test pre-letter teaser tweets. The Sep 7 Swiss Franc tweet (Sunday, 2,412 imp, 2.86% ER) suggests a pre-letter teaser on Sunday/Monday can prime the audience. Try posting one contrarian take from the upcoming letter 12–24 hours before send. If it drives impressions without cannibalizing newsletter opens, make it a fourth regular tweet.
11. Anchor DeFi concepts to TradFi tickers. Sep 15's Pendle/NVDA tweet (7 clicks) and Sep 8's “real Nasdaq stock” tweet (7 clicks) are the two highest-click tweets of the month. The audience responds when DeFi is framed against assets they already know. Make this a deliberate choice in at least one tweet per issue.

Subscriber Trajectory

12. Monitor deliver count decline. The analysis letter dropped from 4,705 delivers (Aug 4) to 4,636 (Sep 15) — 69 fewer subscribers in 6 weeks. At this rate, the letter loses ~180 subscribers by year-end. Investigate: are these paid churns or email bounces? If paid churns, the absence of premium CTAs and cross-links may be contributing. If bounces, run a list-cleaning pass.
