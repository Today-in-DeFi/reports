Headline Analysis

What works (20.5%+ OR)

1. Curiosity + contrarian framing. “DeFi Tokens the Market May Have Mispriced” (21.23%) — the question mark + “mispriced” implies the reader knows something others don’t. This was the best Guide headline of the period.
2. News-hook + number. “Ethereum Glamsterdam is Coming: Mainnet Fees Could Drop 3x” (21.08%) — combines a timely event with a concrete magnitude. Reads like breaking analysis, not an explainer.
3. Outcome + named protocols. “Amplifying Stablecoin LP APY with Leverage: Tangent and LlamaLend v2” (20.98%) — tells you the result (amplify APY) and names specific protocols so it doesn’t feel generic.

What doesn’t work (sub-20% OR)

1. Generic “Here’s How” framing. “Uniswap’s Improved Pools Pay >10% — Here’s How it Works” (19.89%) and “Tokenized Stocks Can Now Earn You Yield — Here’s How” (20.63%) both use the same tired CTA. “Here’s How” signals low-effort content and has appeared twice in recent issues. Stop using it.
2. Vague listicles without tension. “Farm Safer with These 5 DeFi Tools” (19.93%) — no number, no urgency, no “why now.” Reads like a content-mill listicle, not editorial analysis.
3. Too-long headlines that truncate. “After $116M Vanished From ‘Secure’ Wallets: The Actual Checklist for Safe Self-Custody” (18.87%) — 86 characters, most inboxes cut at ~60. The strongest part (“$116M Vanished”) shows but the payoff (“Checklist for Safe Self-Custody”) doesn’t.
4. Mechanism-first, no tension. “Earn 11% Fixed APR From Funding Rates Arbitrage” (20.07%) — leads with the yield claim but “Funding Rates Arbitrage” is jargon that self-selects a narrow audience. No implied risk or urgency.

Content structure observations

TL;DR adoption is working. All recent Guides include a TL;DR box at the top with 4–5 bullets (what, why now, best opportunity, biggest risk, who it’s for). This is good — keep it. The best TL;DRs read like a standalone briefing, not a table of contents.
Topic diversity is the Guide’s strength. The 14 issues span: yield strategies (stablecoins, LPs, options, Uniswap v4), protocol deep-dives (Panoptic, Tangent, LlamaLend), ecosystem maps (perps dexes, Solana yields), security (self-custody), risk tooling (farm safety tools), macro/valuation (mispriced tokens), and new primitives (tokenized stocks, fixed-rate loans, funding rate arbitrage). No other letter covers this range. Lean into this — it’s what makes the Guide distinct.
Best-performing issues share a common structure:
  1. Open with the “hidden truth” — the non-obvious insight that justifies the Guide existing (e.g., “most tokens are down 40–70% but some are still earning”)
  2. Framework or filter — give the reader a decision tool, not just information
  3. Specific opportunities — name venues, rates, links
  4. Risk section — honest, quantitative
Weakest issues skip the “why now” hook. The Self-Custody guide (18.87% OR) opened with background on the Coldcard hack rather than leading with “your seed may be compromised right now.” The Covered Calls guide (19.83% OR) opened with a textbook definition of options rather than the practical edge. Sep 18’s Uniswap guide risks the same trap if it opens with “Uniswap v4 launched new pools” instead of “LPs have been leaking yield to arb bots for years — that just changed.” Frontload the urgency.

General content recommendations

#RecommendationWhy
1 Open with the contrarian insight, not the explainer. First 2 sentences should be: “here’s what the market is getting wrong” or “here’s the window that just opened.” Top 4 Guides all led with a market observation, not a definition
2 Target 2,000–2,800 words. Enough depth to justify the “guide” label, short enough to not signal “homework.” Sweet-spot bucket averaged 20.4% OR; 3,200+ word issues averaged 19.7%
3 End with “What to do right now” — a 3–4 bullet action box, not a risk disclaimer. Readers who scroll to the end are the most engaged — give them a clear next step
4 Cross-link to Farming letter when the Guide covers a yield strategy. 9 of 14 Guides were yield-adjacent — link to that week’s specific farms
5 Add a “who is this for” qualifier in the TL;DR or opening paragraph. Guide topics vary widely — letting readers self-select reduces unsubscribe risk on niche topics

Twitter patterns that work

1. Highest engagement rate: “Direct benefit” framing (avg 4.2% ER).
“Your Curve LP shouldn’t just sit there” (6.58% ER) and “Most DeFi yield comes from the same crowded trades” (3.51% ER). These tell the reader their current behavior is leaving money on the table.
2. Knowledge-gap hooks drive clicks, not just engagement.
Sep 14’s “Everyone checks the APY. Almost nobody checks what’s behind it” delivered 2,219 impressions and 11 clicks — the best click total of the week. The “5 free tools” tweet (788 imp, 8 clicks = 1.0% CTR) had the best click-through rate of any Sep Guide tweet. Knowledge-gap framing consistently converts.
3. Highest impressions: Fear/news hooks (avg 2,590 imp).
“$116M drained from hardware wallets” (2,590 imp) and “A carry trade only works if your cost of funds is known” (3,651 imp with image). Threat-first tweets get amplified, even when the Guide OR was lower.
4. Monday tweets outperform Friday tweets.
Across all Guides with tweet data, the Monday follow-up tweet averaged 1,220 impressions vs. 770 for Friday evening tweets. The Covered Calls Monday tweet (2,844 imp) beat both Friday tweets (579 + 719) combined. Weekend readers discover the letter and are primed for the reminder.

Twitter patterns that don’t work

1. Repeating the headline verbatim. Friday tweets that just restated the subject line averaged 0.6% ER — readers who skipped the email won’t click the same words on Twitter. Reframe the angle.
2. Two similar tweets on the same day. Several Guides had two Friday tweets with near-identical framing. The second consistently got 40–60% fewer impressions. Use the second slot for a different angle (e.g., if tweet 1 is the insight, tweet 2 is a specific number or risk callout).
3. Sep 18 tweets underperformed. All three Sep 18 tweets stayed under 765 impressions. The “Uniswap stablecoin LPs have been leaking yield to arb bots for years” tweet (540 imp, 5 eng, 1 click) wasted the best hook angle by burying it in a low-reach tweet. This should have been the lead tweet, not the third.
