5. Action Items

1. Keep the suffix dropped — validated. Four suffix-free issues now average 20.88% vs 20.33% for five suffixed issues (+0.55pp). This is no longer experimental. Never bring “- Weekly Recap” back.
2. Reframe positive stories as threat-resolved headlines. Sep 14’s “Uniswap Improves Stable LPs APR” (20.03%) would have been stronger as “LPs Were Quietly Losing Money on Uniswap. Not Anymore.” Same content, threat-first framing, under 55 chars. When the week’s biggest story is positive, flip the framing to “the problem that just got fixed.”
3. Target 55 chars or fewer. Two issues under 55 chars average 21.27% OR — 0.67pp above the series average and 1.24pp above Sep 14. The LWID two-topic format naturally pushes over 55 chars; consider leading with one story and using a subtitle or preview text for the second.
4. Use the knowledge-gap tweet format more. “Everyone checks the APY. Almost nobody checks what’s behind it” hit 2,219 impressions and 11 clicks — the best impression count across all LWID tweets and the second-best click count. Write one knowledge-gap tweet per LWID set. Formula: universal behavior + “but almost nobody [does the important thing].”
5. Add a premium CTA to the LWID closing. Zero of 14 LWIDs include one. LWID has the highest and most consistent OR across all letter types. It’s the best vehicle for converting free readers. Even a single line (“Want the full onchain breakdown? Upgrade for $5/mo”) would be worth testing.
6. Add cross-links to other letters. Still zero cross-links after 14 issues. The LWID audience is the most engaged segment — train them to open Tuesday’s Outlook and Thursday’s Farming Guide by cross-linking: “Want the full yield breakdown? Thursday’s Farming Guide covers the best new farms.”
7. Standardize the summary section as “TL;DR”. Four different names have been used across issues. Pick TL;DR and use it every week as the first section after the subscribe boilerplate. Consistency builds reader habit.
8. Investigate the Sep 7 data gap. Sep 7 is still showing 0% OR. If this is a recurring CSV export issue for UTC+8 Monday publishes, consider exporting manually from the Substack dashboard on Tuesday mornings, or shifting publish time earlier to avoid the UTC day boundary.
